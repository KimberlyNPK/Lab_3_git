The color pink:
The world did not begin in fire or shadow, but in a breathless, blushing hue that clung to the edges of the universe like the skin of a ripening peach. Iris had spent forty years cataloging the grays of the concrete city, filing invoices and measuring rain, until the morning the sky decided to bleed spun sugar.

It started with a single drop on her windowsill—not water, but a pigment so vivid it hummed against the glass. It smelled of crushed peony petals and copper, sharp and impossibly sweet. When she touched it, her fingertip bloomed with warmth, a sudden pulse that raced up her arm and settled behind her ribs.

Iris walked to the window and pushed it open, letting the crisp autumn air flood the room. Outside, the transformation was absolute. The stubborn oaks traded their brittle browns for a riot of magenta and cherry blossom, while the asphalt streets softened into the shade of a ballerina’s slipper at twilight. Neighbors poured from their doorways, rubbing their eyes in disbelief as the entire neighborhood bathed in the surreal, glowing aura of bubblegum and neon.

Then the first groan echoed down the block, rattling the neighborhood mailboxes.

Iris blinked, her glowing pink hands freezing mid-gesture as the local supermarket at the end of the street burst open in a tidal wave of the undead. They weren't hunting brains or flesh; instead, hundreds of groaning, pale-skinned zombies clad in faded tracksuits and stained aprons were fiercely hoarding canned peaches, bulk toilet paper, and clearance-tag pastries.

At the front of the shuffling stampede, a decaying former accountant aggressively steered a shopping cart piled ten feet high with discounted frozen waffles, his jaw hanging completely unhinged as he hissed at anyone attempting to pass him in the cereal aisle. Iris watched in stunned silence as the apocalypse officially collided with discount grocery day, realizing with absolute horror that even death couldn't cure humanity's relentless obsession with bulk-buying paper towels.
